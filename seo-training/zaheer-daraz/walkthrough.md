# Five-minute walkthrough: Zaheer Murad

Spoken notes for the Day 10 walkthrough. Read at a steady pace, about five minutes.

I am Zaheer Murad, and my practice site is Daraz Pakistan. I had already done keyword research, but the sheet was not in this workspace, so I did not invent search volumes. I took the focus keywords from the two assigned topics and from what each live page is actually for.

The homepage keyword is “online shopping in Pakistan”. The old title had that idea at the start, which is right, and the brand at the end, which is also right, but the title was 72 characters. The description was the single word “Pakistan”. My replacement is 51 characters, still starts with the keyword, still ends with Daraz, and the description is 145 characters. It tells a shopper to compare sellers and then browse.

The smartphones page is where the first blog has to send people. The live title was exactly 60 characters, but it led with “Mobile Price”. I will not quote prices, so I will not put price in the title either. The new title starts “Buy smartphones in Pakistan” and ends with Daraz. The old description was 192 characters and called the phones the best and the price the best. Mine is 141 characters and points at the seller, the warranty and the return rules.

Returns were already close. The FAQ title and description were 60 and 140, so length was not the problem. The brand was at the front. I moved “returns and refunds” to the start and Daraz to the end. The how-to-return title had no brand at all. It now starts with the task. The Help Center was the weak page: title and description were both “Help Center”, 11 characters. The new title is “Help with orders, payments and returns”, and the description tells you to track a parcel or contact care.

For the blogs, the focus keywords are “choose a smartphone in Pakistan” and “online shopping in Pakistan”. Each meta title starts with that phrase and ends with Daraz. Each description is inside 140 to 155 characters and contains a next step. The slugs repeat the topic in ordinary words.

Inside the articles I put the keyword in the first sentence and in one H2. The smartphone piece uses H3s for the two kinds of day a phone has to survive, then for the listing and the seller. The shopping piece uses H3s under payment, for cash on delivery and for cards and wallets. Both end with an FAQ in H3s. Internal links go to the five audited URLs, plus each blog links to the other.

I checked facts instead of inventing them. From Daraz’s own returns FAQ I used the 14-day window, the original packaging rule, no charge to ship a return, and the option to return only part of an order. From the refund policy I named the payment methods the page itself names, and I said cash-on-delivery refunds are described as going to Daraz Wallet. I did not copy the timing tables, because one number does not fit every method, and the policy says the table does not cover Daraz Global. Where a phone’s storage or network status matters, I told the reader to read the listing. I did not supply a specification.

The draft had the same bones and the wrong shape. The keyword arrived late, some links were bare URLs, and the Help Center was missing. The final pass fixed those and kept both articles between 1,000 and 1,200 words.

If I were marking this against the brief, the audit is the ten marks, these metas are the thirty, the two finals are the forty, and the tracker plus this walkthrough are the deadline and feedback marks. I would ask the trainer two things. First, whether “Daraz” or “Daraz.pk” is the brand they want at the end of a title, because the live site uses both and I picked the shorter one so the keyword could stay in front. Second, whether a keyword sheet will be shared later. I will swap the focus phrases if the sheet names a different wording, and I will still not add a search volume I do not have. That is the submission.
