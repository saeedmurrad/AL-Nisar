# Acne Care Basics: Daily Skin Habits and When to See a Dermatologist

Status: draft. Not the submitted version.

A sensible week of acne care basics has two parts. One is a gentle habit at the sink. The other is a moment when you stop trying new things and let a dermatologist see the skin. Neither part is a promise that the spots will go. A routine that suited someone else can sting you. If your own clinician has already given instructions, those instructions win over this draft.

Nothing here is a dose, a brand you must buy, or a count of clinic visits.

## Stay gentle on purpose

Home care is for not making the skin angrier. The strongest product in the cupboard is often the wrong one, because sore skin is harder to assess later.

Wash with a gentle cleanser and your hands. Skip the rough cloth. Use the rhythm your clinician set, if they set one. Lukewarm water is enough. Very hot water and a hard flannel feel serious and leave tightness behind. Pat the skin dry. If a headscarf, helmet, collar or mask sits on the skin for hours, wash that skin gently too. Do not announce that the fabric caused the spots. Mention it at the appointment and let a trained person weigh it.

Keep your hands off individual spots. Squeezing adds a wound. It is not treatment. Remove makeup before sleep. “Non-comedogenic” on a label is the maker’s hint, not a guarantee, and your clinician can tell you whether makeup belongs in your week at all.

Change one product at a time. Three new things on the same morning make a mystery. If something stings hard, or the skin swells, weeps or becomes very sore, stop and ask. Do not continue because the bottle mentioned purging. Ask a clinician about sun protection instead of copying a number from a forum. Some prescribed or clinic treatments increase sun sensitivity. A fresh pillowcase and a wiped phone are ordinary hygiene. They are not a cure, and continuing spots are not a character flaw.

## The limit of a habit

A habit does not choose an acid, a tablet or a machine. It does not prove a food is guilty. Food questions belong with someone who knows your health. A short diary is fair to bring. Cutting out meals because a caption said so is not.

Two careful weeks that look unchanged are not a failure. Some patterns need training. Switching to a harsher wash out of frustration is how skin becomes both spotty and raw. If you also want hair removed on the same area, do not book both ideas in secret. Say both in one consultation.

## Signs that the sink is not enough

Arrange a proper look if the spots are deep, painful or swelling. Arrange one if marks or pits worry you, or if the pattern returns after you have been gentle. If the skin is hot or weeping, or you feel unwell, get care promptly rather than shopping for another scrub. Do not use someone else’s prescription, and do not buy a strong product just because the bottle looks clinical.

Also book if your sleep, your work or your willingness to go out has changed. You do not need a speech about vanity first. You may also book from plain curiosity, knowing the visit might end in advice and nothing else.

## The first conversation

Expect questions about what you have used, what you take, and how the month goes, plus a look at the skin in ordinary light. The clinician might send you home with a habit, or might talk about clinic care. Ask what they think is happening, in ordinary words. Ask what to do and what to stop. Ask what should bring you back, and what should make you call sooner.

Read the names of the team before you travel if the clinic lists them, but do not confuse a web page with a relationship. Ask how the first visit is arranged, and if any further treatment is mentioned, ask what it is for and what it will not be claimed to do. Leave with the home steps written down. “We will sort it” is not a plan. A promise that one visit ends the problem is a reason to be careful, not a reason to relax.

## What to take into the room

A dermatologist can do more with a clear history than with a speech about how upset you are, though the upset matters and you should say it. Before you go, note when the spots tend to appear, what you have put on the skin in the last month, and which product you stopped because it hurt. If you are comfortable, a photo taken in daylight on a typical day can remind both of you what “usual” looks like. Do not edit the photo. Do not start a new product the night before so the appointment sees a different face from the one you live with.

Write your three questions on paper. In the room it is easy to nod and leave without the home steps. The final version turns this preparation into the consultation section and links the clinic pages a reader would open next. It still does not promise that the visit will clear the skin.

## Questions this draft leaves you with

### Does a gentle wash mean I can skip the dermatologist?

No. The wash only tries to limit irritation. Pain, scarring or real distress are reasons to book. Mild skin that you are happy to watch can be watched, on purpose.

### A spot looks ready to squeeze.

Leave it. Swelling or strong pain is a reason to ask, not a reason to use your thumbs.

### Which foods should I drop?

This draft will not say. Bring a diary if you want the question discussed. A stranger’s meal plan is not care.

### A new product hurt.

Stop. Do not layer a second new product on top. Badly sore, swollen or blistered skin, or feeling unwell, means you should seek care. Slight tightness can rest until the consultation, where you mention it.
