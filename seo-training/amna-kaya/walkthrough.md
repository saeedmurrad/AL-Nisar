# Five-minute walkthrough: Amna

Spoken notes for the Day 10 walkthrough. Read at a steady pace, about five minutes.

I am Amna, and the practice site is Kaya Skin Clinic. The address we were given opens the UAE English site. There is no Pakistan section on that host, so I audited the live UAE pages and I did not pretend the clinic was somewhere else.

I also had no keyword sheet in the workspace, so I did not invent search volumes. The focus keywords come from the assigned topics and from the job of each page: a skin, hair and body clinic; laser hair removal; acne care; about Kaya Clinic; and the dermatology team.

Length was the main fault on the live snippets. The homepage title was 68 characters and started with the brand. The description was 158 characters and spent them on clinic counts and customer counts. Those figures do not even match other text on the homepage, which showed different numbers. I deleted every count. The new title starts with “skin, hair and body clinic” and ends with Kaya Clinic. The description is 149 characters and asks the reader to book a consultation.

The laser title was 82 characters and promised long-term hair reduction and smooth skin. The description was 187 characters and repeated that promise. Mine is “Laser hair removal: questions to ask”. It offers a consultation and says the page is not a promise. The acne title was 79 characters and talked about solutions. The description was 213 characters and teased “how many sessions you may need”. I will not invent a session count. The new description tells the reader to learn the basics and book. About-us led with a customer figure and repeated the brand. Experts called the team the best and missed a space before the pipe. Both now lead with the topic and end with the brand, inside 60 and inside 140 to 155.

The blog keywords are “laser hair removal” and “acne care basics”. Each meta title starts with the keyword and ends “Kaya Clinic”. Each description asks the reader to book, and neither says the skin will change. The slugs are the topics in full.

On the page, the laser article uses the keyword in the first words and in the H2 “before laser hair removal”. During and after are their own H2s. H3s cover what to bring and what to ask. The acne article opens on the keyword, then uses H2s for daily skin habits and for when to see a dermatologist. H3s cover washing and hands. Both articles end in an FAQ. Internal links go to the five audited pages, and each blog links to the other so a reader with both questions can move without searching.

I did not copy the clinic’s treatment claims. I did not say a visit is painless, permanent, or guaranteed. I did not ban a food or name a dose. Where a timetable matters, the article tells the reader to follow the clinician’s sheet. The one firm safety line is to seek care promptly if skin is hot, weeping, or the person feels unwell, and not to share someone else’s prescription.

The drafts had the same boundaries and a weaker shape. The keyword sat too late, and there were no internal links. The final pass added the links, moved the keyword forward, and kept each article between 1,000 and 1,200 words.

On the marking split, the five-page audit is the ten points, the titles and descriptions are the thirty, the two finals are the forty, and showing up with the tracker and this walkthrough is the twenty for deadlines and feedback. I would ask the trainer one thing before anyone publishes: the greeting says Ammara and the brief says Amna, and I have filed the work under Amna. I would also ask whether the brand line must be “Kaya Skin Clinic” in full. I used “Kaya Clinic”, which is what several live titles already say, because the full name pushes a keyword off the front of a 60-character title. I will not add a result claim to buy those characters back. That is what I would walk through on day 10.
